In June 2024, suspected fireworks set Gregory Canyon on fire — just west of University Hill.If fireworks go off at your house and you’re there, Boulder law says you must try to stop them — or you can be treated as a complicitor.

Fireworks are part of the noise problem in our neighborhoods — and a fire risk in a town that sits against wildfire country. In Boulder every one of them is illegal, sparklers included.

Since July 2024, an independent acoustic sensor network on University Hill has located nearly 400 fireworks-like blasts with high confidence — in every month of the year, and nearly three in four between 9 p.m. and 2 a.m.

Fireworks and Boulder’s wildfire zone

Each dot is a fireworks blast located by the sensor network; the shaded parcels are the city’s own Wildland-Urban Interface — where Boulder’s wildfire building code applies. Nearly two in three located blasts (248 of 396) went off inside it. That is a floor: the zone is drawn parcel by parcel and leaves out streets, where many fireworks are set off.

What the city’s own records show

Boulder police log fireworks complaints under their own call code. In the city’s public calls-for-service data:

1,958fireworks complaints to Boulder police, January 2023 – September 2026
43%came from within a 15-minute walk of CU Boulder — home to 49% of residents; July’s citywide spike pulls this down
60%of those near campus came outside the weeks around the Fourth of July (June 25–July 10) and New Year’s Eve
3 in 5Boulder fireworks complaints in August and September came from near campus, 2023–2026 (45% to 80%, year to year)

About half of Boulder’s residents live within that 15-minute walk, so near-campus fireworks aren’t outsized in June and July, when the whole city lights up. In most other months they are: across August and September 2023–2026, more than three of every five fireworks complaints in Boulder came from the neighborhoods around campus, though the share swings widely from year to year. Year-round, 63% of near-campus fireworks complaints come in between 10 p.m. and 3 a.m.

Complaints are only the part that gets reported. On 131 nights since July 2024 the sensors located at least one high-confidence blast on the Hill; on 84 of them, police logged no fireworks complaint from that area. Most of those were nights with a single blast. On busier nights, with three or more, a complaint was logged on all but 6 of 31.

A fire risk, not just a noise problem

  • Most of University Hill is inside Boulder’s wildfire zone. The city’s own wildfire code map puts most of the Hill inside its Wildland-Urban Interface — the area where new building must meet ignition-resistant standards — in its least strict class, and the strictest classes begin right along the Hill’s western and southern edges, toward the foothills.
  • It has already happened here. On June 24, 2024, a fire suspected to have been started by fireworks burned in Gregory Canyon at the base of Flagstaff Road, just west of the Hill. Police found a 21-year-old suspect sitting at 12th Street and Baseline Road — on the Hill itself — and arrested him for second-degree arson. A month later, juveniles setting off fireworks were followed by a grass fire at Casey Middle School.
  • Fire season is every season. Boulder County’s two most alarming recent fires came in winter and spring: the wind-driven Marshall Fire on December 30, 2021, which destroyed more than 1,000 homes, and the NCAR Fire, which prompted evacuations across south Boulder on March 26, 2022. A dry, windy November night on the Hill is not a safe night for fireworks.

The law

  • Every firework is illegal in Boulder — including sparklers and fountains. Possessing, selling, storing, or setting them off is prohibited anywhere in city limits; apart from narrow exclusions (railroad signals, police and military use), only permitted public displays are exempt (B.R.C. 5-6-6). Colorado allows some “permissible” fireworks elsewhere, but state law lets cities ban them, and Boulder does (C.R.S. 24-33.5-2007).
  • Up to $2,650 and 90 days in jail per violation (B.R.C. 5-2-4; the fine cap is indexed to inflation, and jail has limits for minors and vicarious liability).
  • Every resident at the house is on the hook. When someone sets off fireworks on a residential property, each resident present nearby has a legal duty to take reasonable steps to stop it and, if that fails, to help police identify who did it. A resident who doesn’t is treated as a complicitor (B.R.C. 5-6-6(c)).
  • If it starts a fire, it can be arson. The Gregory Canyon fire above ended in a second-degree arson arrest. For CU students, a fireworks violation can also mean a referral to Student Conduct.

Who else hears it

Fireworks sold to the public, measured at the minimum safe distance for the person setting them off (8–15 meters), peaked at about 138–141 dB — right at the 140 dB peak limit U.S. and EU workplace rules set for impulse noise. In a survey of 1,225 dogs, about half showed at least some fear of fireworks, and a few took weeks or months to recover. GPS-tracked wild geese flew 5 to 16 kilometers farther on New Year’s Eve and fed more for at least the 11 days the study followed them afterward. And VA medical centers warn that fireworks can trigger PTSD symptoms in veterans, especially those whose trauma involved explosions or gunfire.

What you can do

  1. If you see fire, or sparks landing in dry grass or brush, call 911.
  2. Otherwise, report fireworks to Boulder police non-emergency at 303-441-3333. Each call is logged and becomes part of the public record — the record that shows where and when this happens. How to make a call count →
  3. Have fireworks you shouldn’t? CU Boulder says unused fireworks can be dropped off, no penalty, at the City of Boulder Public Safety Building, 1805 33rd St.
